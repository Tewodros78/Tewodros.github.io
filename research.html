Conflict Escalation and Acute Food Insecurity in Ethiopia: Difference-in-Differences Analysis

This paper examines how conflict escalation affects acute food insecurity across Ethiopian administrative regions from January 2010 to December 2023. Using a monthly sub-national panel of 1,848 region-month observations and a dynamic Difference-in-Differences approach that exploits the differential timing of conflict escalation across regions, we find that areas with significant escalation experienced an average increase of about 0.611 IPC food insecurity phase units relative to stable regions and the pre-escalation period. The effect emerged gradually, peaked during the intense civil war years (2021–2022), and began to decline after the Pretoria Cessation of Hostilities Agreement in November 2022, although food insecurity remained considerably high until late 2023.

Commodity Price Shocks and Inflation Dynamics in Ethiopia: A Machine LearningApproach

This study examines how commodity price volatility on inflation in Ethiopia, using time-series data from 1992 to 2022. The research employs machine learning models, including XGBoost, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting, as well as an Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression model to analyze both global and domestic shocks that influence inflation. The findings indicate that oil price growth, lagged domestic inflation, and global inflation are the most significant predictors of domestic inflation, underscoring Ethiopia's vulnerability to global commodity price changes and the persistence of inflationary trends. The Gradient Boosting model demonstrated superior predictive accuracy, explaining over 64% of the variation in inflation.
